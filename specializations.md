# Specializations — Design Document

None of these are classes. They are profiles that emerge from the same base mechanics when a character invests their stats and skills in a specific direction and develops a particular approach to channeling. Two characters with the same "specialization" will still be different because channel quality is individual and accumulated knowledge is personal.

---

## The Rogue Path — Instability

Any mage can drain external sources — objects, vegetation, animals, people — to instantly lower their own EP. It is not a class feature. It is a choice, repeatable, with accumulated consequences.

Energy extracted from living systems is not clean Ether. It is noise. It works, but it introduces **Instability** into the channel.

**Instability Tracker** — separate from EP, 0 to 100:

| Range | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Clean channel |
| 20–40 | Background noise — minor penalties to Coherence |
| 40–60 | Scarred channel — progressive loss of precision |
| 60–80 | Active degradation — loss of complex effects, involuntary discharges under stress |
| 80–99 | Probable point of no return — only high-Magnitude effects, no Complexity |
| 100 | The end. In the most spectacular way possible |

**Permanent damage threshold:** exceeding 60 Instability for prolonged periods leaves irreversible scars on the channel. The character never returns to zero. They can stabilize, but with an unbreakable ceiling that wasn't there before.

**Perverse advantage of direct draining:** it bypasses ambient thermal drop. The balance is paid by the drained source, not the area. The physical alert signal other mages use to detect channeling does not manifest. A high-Instability rogue is harder to "read."

**Instability as a dual resource:**

High instability = worse control BUT higher energy ceiling. The "dirty" channel is noisy but powerful. The player always has a real choice: control or raw power.

At very high instability the mage loses Complexity first, then Duration. Only raw high-Magnitude effects remain — they are not a more powerful mage, they are a geological event with opinions. They can still do enormous things but not precise ones.

### The Symptom System

The rogue player **never sees the number**. They see symptoms.

The list is public — it exists in the lore as more or less well-known legend. Understanding what is happening to a specific individual requires a Knowledge roll. An experienced mage who directly observes the character can have more precise information than the character themselves has about their own condition.

**Progressive symptoms (public list):**
1. Nightmares, disturbed sleep
2. Unstable body temperature — always cold or hot without reason
3. Nosebleeds under magical exertion
4. Animals becoming agitated or fleeing in their presence
5. Tremors in precision actions — difficulty writing, handling small objects
6. Involuntary discharges under acute emotional stress
7. Brief hallucinations at the edge of vision
8. Loss of periods of time

**The curve is hidden:** the exact progression — at which numerical threshold each symptom manifests — is determined at character creation with a secret GM roll, and varies from individual to individual. The curve exists complete, in advance, in a sealed envelope. The character discovers it by living it. The GM does not modify it mid-campaign.

**Who recognizes what:**

| Observer | Available information |
|---|---|
| Anyone | Something is wrong. Better to stay away |
| Base Knowledge roll | These are the symptoms of a mage losing control |
| High roll / experienced mage | Rough estimate of where they are in the progression |
| Rogue mage with direct experience | Nuances others can't read. Perhaps things the character themselves doesn't know |

## The Monk as Counter-Mage

The monk has spent years perceiving the Ether with extreme finesse — reading another channel's pattern is the natural extension of that discipline. It is the most coherent defensive specialization for the priest/hermit profile.

**Three operational levels:**

**Reading** — perceives that someone is channeling before the effect manifests. Passive Wisdom roll. Enormous tactical advantage: they know what's about to happen before it happens.

**Interference** — disrupts another's pattern during channeling. Increases the target's Coherence DC. Doesn't block them, destabilizes them. Requires total concentration — the monk does nothing else that round.

**Active dissonance** — deliberately attempts to be out of phase with the opponent's channel. If successful, every cast by the target has increased failure probability. If the phase is wrong, it amplifies instead of blocking. Requires having read the channel beforehand — it cannot be improvised against someone never observed.

**The most narratively interesting thing:** a monk operating this way shows nothing externally. No gestures, no components, no visible thermal drop. To those who can't read channels, the opposing mage appears to be failing casts through bad luck. Only another mage understands what is actually happening.

**Stat profile:** Wisdom as absolute priority, off the charts. Medium Constitution to sustain prolonged concentration. Intelligence useful but not essential — they don't build complex patterns, they read and disrupt them.

## The Social Manipulator

Social skills at maximum grade already do powerful things — refined psychological manipulation, behavior reading, calibrated pressure. The natural limit is that the target maintains their own judgment, even if compromised.

A mage with sufficient Mastery can add a second layer: direct biological manipulation of the target while working them socially. Lowering cortisol, raising oxytocin, flooding the system with hormones that produce trust and compliance. They are not overwriting consciousness — they are tuning biology to be maximally receptive to what is being said verbally and behaviorally.

The result from the outside looks like charm. Technically it is social skill pushed to the limit + precise biological manipulation on an active nervous system.

**The natural gates — all three are required together:**
- **High Presence** — must be credible and magnetic without magic
- **Social skill at peak** — knowing what to say, when, how
- **Sufficient Mastery** — maintaining a complex biological pattern on a reacting target while also speaking

It is temporary by definition — stop maintaining the pattern and biology returns to normal.

**The gradient:**

| Level | What happens | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Pure skill | Convincing, target can still say no | No EP |
| Skill + mild biology | Very hard to resist, self still present | Low EP, medium Mastery |
| Skill + heavy biology | Total temporary compliance | High EP, high Mastery |
| Permanent biology | Progressive deterioration of target | Unsustainable EP, irreversible |

The permanent zombie exists as a theoretical endpoint but requires continuous EP on a biological system that is actively deteriorating under that pressure. You can have someone in your service for hours, maybe days. Then something breaks irreversibly in the target.

**The most disturbing part:** when the effect ends the target remembers everything. No gaps, no altered memories. They have done things they cannot explain, with an emotional context they no longer feel. The mage has not technically controlled anyone's mind — they have just made it impossible to say no for a while.

## The Illusionist

The monk reads others' perceptive patterns. The illusionist writes false patterns into those same systems. It is the same ability used in the opposite direction.

**How illusions work:**

The brain does not perceive reality directly — it processes signals arriving from the senses. An illusion does not create anything real: it intercepts or overwrites those signals before they reach conscious processing. The target sees the wall that isn't there because their optic nerves are receiving signals consistent with a wall. It is not mind magic — it is sensory sabotage.

**Complexity by sensory layer:**

Each added sensory channel increases the Coherence DC. A partial illusion is fragile — using the uncovered sense breaks it.

| Layer | Additional DC | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Visual only | Base | Breaks on touch |
| Visual + auditory | +3 | Breaks on smell or touch |
| Visual + auditory + olfactory | +6 | Nearly convincing |
| Full multisensory | +10 | Enormously costly, nearly impossible to sustain |

An illusion that responds coherently to the target's actions — the fake wall that feels solid when touched — is the highest Complexity level in the system. Not just projecting, but updating the pattern in real time in response to external inputs.

**The non-human biology limit:**

The illusionist has mapped the human perceptive system. Against creatures with different senses it's a gamble — it might not work at all, might produce something irrelevant to their primary senses, might work by accident on channels they didn't expect.

**Stat profile:** High Intelligence to build and update complex patterns in real time. High Wisdom to perceive what the target is perceiving and know what to overwrite. Medium Constitution — not as physically demanding as thermal magic, but prolonged concentration has a cost on EP.

**The most dangerous combination in the system:**

An illusionist working with a social manipulator. One lowers critical defenses by altering biology, the other overwrites what the target perceives. The target has no internal tools to understand what is happening — their body says everything is fine and their senses confirm a coherent reality. Only a mage with sufficient Wisdom nearby can read that something is off in the patterns. For everyone else there is no detectable signal.

# Magic System — Design Document

---

## Part I — The World and Magic

### The Ether

Everything that exists is permeated by an invisible, omnipresent background field: the **Ether**. It is not mystical energy in the romantic sense — it's closer to an electromagnetic field or cosmic background radiation: physical, measurable by those who know how, indifferent to human existence.

The Ether in its natural state is chaotic, incoherent, white noise. It has no intention. It has no morality. It simply exists.

### The Mage

In rare individuals, the biological channel between self and Ether is traversable. It is not a gift — it is a structural anomaly, like having perfect pitch or a photographic memory. It is not chosen, it is discovered. Often in the worst possible way.

A mage does not *create* energy. A mage does not *destroy* anything. They **collimate the Ether** — transforming chaotic noise into coherent, directed patterns, the way a laser works with light. Mastery is not about how much energy you can move, but how little you waste in the process.

The mage is a channel, not a source.

### The Equilibrium Principle

Ether is neither created nor destroyed. Every energy transformation has a cost, and that cost must be paid somewhere.

When a mage collimates the Ether, they extract energy from the surrounding environment. The process is never perfectly isolated — just as an engine emits heat beyond its useful work, magical channeling disperses energy in the immediate vicinity of the practitioner. The most visible signal is the **temperature drop in the area**: even the most experienced masters experience this phenomenon, because it is a physically inevitable consequence of the transformation, not a technical error.

This local entropy has real geographic and strategic consequences:
- Cold climates increase operational risk for the mage and everyone nearby
- Massive, prolonged use can leave permanent scars on the landscape — zones of anomalous heat where energy was discharged, dead land where it was fully extracted

### No Classes — Only Choices

There are no classes in the traditional sense. A warrior, a thief, a mage, a priest — these are descriptions of how an individual has chosen to develop themselves, not mechanical categories with rigid boundaries. The mechanics don't change based on what you call the character. What changes is what they've trained, what they can do, and the choices they've made over time.

This doesn't impoverish the setting — it liberates it. Discretization, where needed, is handled by narrative. The system supports it without imposing it.

### The Priest

A priest is a mage who has chosen monasticism, hermitage, a life of contemplation and channel control as spiritual practice. Closer to a Buddhist monk than a vestal virgin — the discipline of body and mind as a tool for refining Ether perception and minimizing the chaos of channeling.

The practical result is an extremely controlled channel: minimal waste, reduced thermal drop, the ability to maintain complex patterns better than anyone else. They never do anything brutally energetic — it's not what they've trained for, and they probably lack the Constitution to sustain it. But they are the hardest to push out of control, the most efficient in situations requiring finesse, and the most equipped to read other channels.

**The gods** in this system — if they exist as entities distinct from human interpretation — might be patterns sufficiently stable and vast in the Ether to passively influence the channels of those nearby, physically or spiritually. Or it might be what priests tell themselves to give meaning to what they do. The system does not answer this question. It's richer that way.

Legends exist. They are passed down in writings more or less shared, more or less distorted. What people decide to believe about the Ether, its origin, its meaning — depends on lore, cultural context, the history of whoever is speaking. It is profoundly human, and it adds depth to the setting rather than removing it.

### Ethics and Choices

There are no good or evil mages in any absolute sense. There are choices about **where the cost of equilibrium is paid**.

A mage who extracts energy from the inanimate environment around them pays in terms of personal physical stress and area impact. A mage who drains living sources — vegetation, animals, people — offloads the cost onto other systems, which are damaged or die in proportion. It is technically more efficient. It is ethically a choice with precise consequences.

There is no School of Good Magic and School of Evil Magic. There is an individual, a moment, a decision about who or what pays the bill. The rest is narrative.

### Knowledge

Understanding of the Ether is fragmented, incomplete, and mostly passed down through near-tribal oral tradition. Mages are rare, often hunted or regarded with suspicion, and knowledge dies with them if not transmitted.

**The Master** — when one exists — does not teach spells. They recognize the channel in a student (often because they *feel* them as annoying interference), and teach them how not to die: discipline, meditation, pattern control, and whatever they know about components. What they know is partial and personal. Two students of the same master will develop different and incompatible abilities.

**Components** are substances or materials in which Ether charges have been stored — through alchemical rituals not fully understood: berries, bark, minerals, organic matter. Nobody knows exactly *why* certain things work as containers. Nobody knows with certainty how they interact with each other, or how far those interactions become dangerous. Component research is the most active — and most lethal — field of applied magic.

**A mage's Tome** is not a universal grimoire. It's a personal notebook: rituals, proportions, observations, failures. It might be incomprehensible to another mage with different training, or it might be the key to understanding something no one else has ever formalized. Its value is unpredictable and always contextual.

**Institutes** — where they exist, mostly at the courts of the powerful — are places of component research and attempts to codify knowledge. Some things emerge, some are kept secret, some leak out. Knowledge functions like open-source software at its worst: fragments without context, incompatible versions, uncertain attribution.

## Part II — Mechanics

### Character Creation

No classes exist. No list of archetypes to choose from. A character is defined by three overlapping layers: base stats, acquired skills, and a narrative background agreed upon with the GM.

#### Base Stats — Point Buy

Six stats, a fixed point budget distributed freely at creation. No stat is forbidden to anyone — choices reflect the character, not a class.

| Stat | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | Physical power, carry weight, melee |
| **Agility** | Speed, dodging, motor precision |
| **Constitution** | Physical endurance, recovery, damage tolerance and magical strain |
| **Intelligence** | Reasoning, memory, learning capacity and pattern complexity |
| **Wisdom** | Perception, context reading, intuition and Ether sensitivity |
| **Presence** | Charisma, authority, capacity to influence |

A warrior-type character will focus points in Strength, Constitution, Agility. A mage in Wisdom, Intelligence, Constitution. But nothing prevents a warrior with high Wisdom who doesn't yet know why they have it — though the GM does.

#### Skills — Specific Training

Skills are the second level of differentiation. They derive from a base stat but are acquired separately — at creation through background, during the campaign through experience. Concrete training: *Weapon Combat*, *Stealth*, *Arcane Knowledge*, *Component Rituals*, *Medicine*, *Persuasion*.

Two characters with identical stats but different skills are completely different characters.

#### Background — Who You Were Before

The background is the narrative agreement with the GM about what happened before the story began. It doesn't assign stats, but it assigns a starting skill package consistent with the character's history. It also answers the most important question for magical channels: *do you already know?*

**Three possible scenarios for the channel:**

**Recognized and trained** — a master found you, you studied, you know what you are. You start with basic magical skills, understand the concept of EP and its risks, probably have some components and an initial notebook. You know the channel. You don't know its exact quality.

**Unexplained incidents** — you've had inexplicable episodes in the past. You accidentally destroyed something, nearly killed someone, heard things others didn't. You know something is happening, not what it is. No magical skills at the start, but the GM already knows what your channel looks like and when it might manifest again.

**No awareness** — potential intact and completely unknown. It might never manifest during the campaign. It might explode in your face during a moment of extreme stress. It is a narrative variable the GM manages in silence.

In all three cases, channel quality is determined by the GM at creation, regardless of how aware the character is of it. The background determines awareness, not potential.

### Ether Pressure (EP)

The core mechanic is a single gauge per mage: **Ether Pressure**. It rises when channeling, falls with rest. It is not mana — it is the metabolic cost of the channeling process. It represents how much stress the channel is under, regardless of how efficient the transformation is.

| Zone | EP | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Stable** | 0–30% | Clean channeling. Thermal drop: narrative only |
| **Strain** | 30–60% | Mechanical thermal drop — area temperature drops one step per cast |
| **Danger** | 60–90% | Every cast requires a Coherence Roll. Failure = backlash |
| **Critical** | 90%+ | Another cast may kill the mage. Or worse |

**Recovery:** approximately 10% EP per hour of rest. No overnight reset. A mage who pushed hard yesterday is still at risk today.

---

### How Energy Extraction Works

The channeling process creates a **negative pressure** in the local Ether field. The energy required for the effect is extracted from the surrounding environment — and this manifests as a temperature drop in the area. It is not waste: it *is* the extraction itself. The environment is the source, not collateral damage.

The balance is:

```
Energy in effect + Inefficiency dispersal = Total extracted from environment
```

**Dispersal** is the fraction the channel cannot cleanly convert — no transformation is 100% efficient. The less skilled the mage, the higher this fraction, the greater the total extraction for the same effect, the more the area cools.

**EP and thermal drop are two separate things:**
- **EP** = how tired the channel gets operating as a pump
- **Thermal drop** = how dirty the transformation is, how much energy disperses in the process

Even the best mage pays EP. Even the best mage produces some thermal drop. No engine is 100% efficient.

**Thermal drop — environmental scale:**
```
Hot → Temperate → Cold → Freezing → Lethal
```
The drop affects everyone in the area, not just the mage.

**Thermal drop radius — calculable:**

```
Radius (meters) = (EP spent / Mastery modifier) × 0.5
```

Mastery compresses the extraction radius — a skilled mage draws from a smaller, more concentrated volume of environment. It doesn't reduce the total extraction the effect needs; it reduces the area involved.

| Example | EP spent | Mastery | Radius |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veth (Mastery +2), medium cast | 20% | 2 | 5 meters |
| Muzgash (Mastery +1), large cast | 35% | 1 | 17 meters |

### Describing an Effect — The Four Axes

No spell list exists. The mage describes the desired effect. The GM reads the four axes and determines EP cost and Coherence DC.

| Axis | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnitude** | localized dart | plasma sphere, 10m radius |
| **Duration** | instantaneous | sustained pattern over n turns |
| **Complexity** | heat transfer (entropy-friendly) | autonomous structured behavior |
| **Distance** | touch | tens of meters |

*Plasma sphere burning everything in a 10-meter radius* — High Magnitude, Low Duration, Low Complexity (heat is entropy, you're accelerating chaos), Medium Distance. High EP, medium DC. A single brutal effort, then it's over.

*Creature fighting at your side for 5 turns* — Medium Magnitude, High Duration, Extremely High Complexity (ordered pattern actively fighting entropy every turn). EP rises every turn, high DC every turn. If concentration breaks mid-way, the pattern collapses — and the energy has to go somewhere.

Complexity is the most penalizing axis. Destruction is easy. Sustained order is costly and dangerous.

### Mastery

Mastery is not a stat on the character sheet. It is an **emergent value** from three base stats, with a hidden multiplier representing the individual quality of the channel.

| Stat | What it represents for magic |
|---|---|
| **Constitution** | Bearing the physical cost — thermal drop, prolonged strain, channel stress |
| **Wisdom** | Ether perception, modulation finesse, reading other channels |
| **Intelligence** | Complexity of patterns that can be conceptualized and maintained |

```
Mastery = average(CON, WIS, INT) × channel_quality
```

**Channel quality** is the hidden multiplier — determined by the GM at character creation, never directly revealed. The character discovers it over time by noticing that equal effort produces different results than expected. Two characters identical on paper can have completely different magical potential.

Anyone can have a channel — most too weak to matter, some open enough to learn, very few born with off-the-charts conductance. It is not a binary wall. It is a spectrum.

Sufficiently traumatic lore events can modify the threshold — massive Ether exposure, accidentally ingesting a component, being in the area of a lich during a channeling event. The channel opens forcibly. It might close again. It might not.

### Coherence Roll

```
d20 + Mastery vs DC (assigned by GM based on the axes)
```

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Success** | Works |
| **Partial success** | Works, but extra EP / imprecise effect / increased thermal drop |
| **Failure** | Backlash — energy goes where you didn't choose |

**Backlash distribution:**

| Gap (DC − roll) | On caster | In area | Area radius |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 80% | 20% | 1–2 meters |
| 4–7 | 50% | 50% | proportional to EP spent |
| 8+ | 20% | 80% | full radius of attempted cast |

Minor failure — the mage still absorbs most of it. Catastrophic failure — the pattern completely collapsed before the channel could contain anything; energy explodes outward. A mage who fails catastrophically in an enclosed space is a problem for everyone present regardless of faction or intentions.

### Components

A used component lowers the EP cost of a cast — energy comes from the external source instead of the environment. For small casts it can zero out the EP requirement entirely.

Components are consumed. They are rare. Combining them produces effects not entirely predictable — and potentially catastrophic. No mage knows everything about components. Everyone pretends to know more than they do.

**Components as pharmacology — biological compatibility:**

Components are not pure magic. They are Ether stored in biological or chemical structures that already interact with the physical world in ways not completely understood. When introduced into another biology — ingested, applied, absorbed — they add a variable no one can model with certainty.

A generic preparation works on approximately 70% of people. On the remaining 30% it does nothing, or worse. The person preparing it doesn't know until they see it. That's already valuable compared to nothing.

A healer who knows you — who has already treated you, who has recorded your reactions — can prepare something calibrated to your specific biology. They are not a potion vendor: they are closer to a personal physician. Rare. Courts of the powerful have one. Everyone else manages as best they can.

A mage's biology is further complicated by the channel — those who channel regularly have a slightly altered physiology compared to non-mages. A generic preparation on a mage may interact with Ether already present in the channel in ways no generalist healer can estimate.

| Scenario | Effect |
|---|---|
| Known component + known biology | Works as expected, full effect |
| Known component + unknown biology | CON roll of target vs variable DC |
| Unknown component | The GM knows things the player doesn't. Always. |
| Mage as target | Current EP as additional variable on the DC |

### Damage, Incapacitation and Death

| HP | State |
|---|---|
| 1+ | Standing and operational |
| 0 | Incapacitated — down, unconscious or immobilized |
| Negative | Dying — loses 1 HP per round automatically |
| − equal to own CON | Dead |

Stabilizing a character at 0 HP requires an action and a Medicine roll from an ally. In cold conditions the timer accelerates: every temperature step below Cold adds 1 HP of automatic loss per round to incapacitated characters.

### The Mage in Combat

A mage is not defined by magic — they are defined by how they've invested their stats and skills. Nothing prevents combat skills or adequate Strength. In practice, those who have invested heavily in Wisdom and Intelligence have sacrificed something elsewhere — but it's not a rule imposed by the system, it's a consequence of choices.

Typical equipment for a self-aware mage:
- **Heavy insulating clothing** — they know they get cold, and so does everyone nearby
- **No metal armor** — conducts cold too efficiently
- **Emergency heat source** — warming stone, a thermal component
- **A light weapon** — for moments when magic is not the right option

A mage with self-awareness uses magic when it's needed. Not every round, not for every problem. Those who understand this are dangerous. Those who don't are an unpinned grenade in the party's hands.

### Buffs and Nerfs

They exist, but they are not spells — they are applications of the same axes with specific costs and risks.

**Buffs:** you are creating an ordered pattern on a moving target with its own biology. High Duration, extremely High Complexity. Among the most EP-expensive operations that exist. A buff sustained for multiple rounds is often more dangerous for the mage than useful for the target. Biological compatibility applies here too — a buff on incompatible biology can have effects opposite to those intended.

**Nerf on a non-mage:** you are perturbing biology or mechanics — extracting muscular heat, weighing down the air around them, interfering with coordination. High Complexity, morally questionable, it works.

**Nerf on a mage:** the hardest thing in the system. Requires first reading the opponent's pattern — Wisdom roll — then attempting to interfere with an opposing frequency. If you're out of phase with their channel, the risk is amplifying their cast instead of blocking it. Very few mages attempt it, and only those with high Wisdom who know the opponent well do so with any confidence.

### The Lich

A stable node in the Ether where a human being used to be.

The lich has no separate EP gauge because it is no longer separate from the Ether — it has become a coherent pattern in the field itself. It is not alive. It cannot return to being something different. The cost of the transition was the payment of the energetic delta required: an entire region, annihilated. Not for maintenance — for the transition itself.

It is irreversible. It is rare by definition — it requires resources and desperation on an unimaginable scale. See the dedicated lore document for full treatment.

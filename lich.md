# The Lich — Lore Document

*This document contains no combat mechanics. Not because the lich is "immune" to anything — but because the category of combat simply does not apply. What follows describes what a lich is, what its existence means in the world, and what kinds of experiences it generates in its surroundings.*

---

## What a Lich Is

A stable node in the Ether where a human being used to be.

The lich is not alive. It is not dead. It is a pattern of Ether coherence so vast and so old that it exists on a temporal and energetic scale incompatible with that of mortal beings. It does not have motivations comprehensible in a human sense — or rather, it might, but the framework through which it processes them no longer has anything in common with those who observe it.

The cost of the transition was the payment of the energetic delta required to anchor that pattern in physical reality: an entire region, annihilated. Not for maintenance — for the transition itself. What remained does not remember having made a choice. Or perhaps it does, but the emotional meaning of that memory belongs to someone who no longer exists.

A lich outlives civilizations. It traverses geological eras. What a human being calls "history" is, for an ancient lich, a sequence of marginal details — if it notices them at all.

---

## Rarity as a Cosmological Datum

A lich is not a monster. It is not an antagonist with a castle and dungeons. It is a phenomenon — like a volcano, like a tectonic fault, like a star.

If humanity in its entire history remembers the existence of one lich, that is already a great deal. If it remembers two, it is extraordinary. The probability that anyone living has ever seen a lich and survived long enough to transmit a coherent account is close to zero.

What exists are:
- Geographic scars no one can explain — zones where the Ether behaves wrongly, where animals won't enter, where thermal drop is present even without any mage nearby
- Legends layered upon legends, filtered through centuries of religious and cultural interpretation
- Tomes citing other tomes citing oral accounts citing dreams
- Beliefs and rituals entire communities have developed to coexist with something they don't understand, some of which work for reasons no one in the community can articulate

Knowledge of what a lich is has been lost and rediscovered dozens of times. Each time it is partial, contaminated, different from the previous version. No continuous tradition of lich study has survived long enough to become one.

## Two Narrative Scenarios

### The Lich as Ancient Legend

The lich already exists. How long it has done so, no one knows with certainty. Its presence manifests as chronic distortion of local Ether — something nearby communities have learned to call by different names: the Presence, the Silence, the Wrong Place.

A party encountering it in this scenario is a spectator of something that exists on a scale that does not include them. The lich does not notice them any more than an ocean notices a boat. The danger is not hostility — it is physical proximity to something whose mere existence distorts perceivable reality.

The objective is never destruction. It is surviving the encounter and returning with information no living human possesses. That is the most precious treasure the setting can generate.

### The Birth of a Primordial Horror

This is the scale at which the lich should be read in this system.

The party does not go to investigate something that already exists. The party finds itself in the wrong place at the wrong time, and witnesses the birth of something with no precedent in living memory. What they experience that night will become the legend someone else will read a thousand years later in a tome, without fully understanding it.

They cannot stop it. They may not even understand what they are watching while it happens.

## What Is Perceived During Formation

Not an explosion. Not a visually dramatic moment with special effects. Something more subtle and more terrible.

**For a mage in the party:**

The local Ether begins to change. Not violently — *wrongly*. As if physics were acquiring a curvature it didn't have before. The thermal drop normally perceived when someone casts becomes something different — not localized, not temporary. Permanent. Growing. The mage's channel tries to calibrate on a field that is no longer what it was before and doesn't know how. Like trying to tune an instrument while someone rewrites the laws of acoustics.

**For a non-mage:**

First the animals. Then silence — not an absence of sound, but something that occupies the space where sound should be. Then a physical sensation with no name: not cold, not hot, not pain, not fear in the normal sense. Something the nervous system cannot classify and therefore does not process correctly. Some vomit. Some cry without knowing why. Some cannot move.

**If the party knows the person becoming a lich:**

They might still be recognizable for a moment. They still use their memories as an interface. They still respond to their name. But what responds is no longer the person you knew — it is something that inhabits those memories in a completely different way, like someone wearing another person's clothes and not understanding why they don't fit.

Then it stops responding. Not because it leaves. Because you have become irrelevant.

## Trauma as Consequence

A character who witnesses the formation of a lich does not emerge unchanged. Not in the sense of HP lost or stats reduced — in the sense that their relationship with perceivable reality is no longer the same.

**For a mage:**

They have perceived through their channel something the channel was not built to perceive. Like looking directly at the sun — you don't see more, you see less, and for a variable amount of time you don't see well at all. The channel may require weeks to recalibrate. It may never fully recalibrate. It may recalibrate on something different from before — a slightly altered frequency the mage cannot describe but that other very attentive mages might notice.

**For a non-mage with a latent channel:**

That event may have opened the channel — not in a controlled way, not in a pleasant way. In a way that will require years to understand what happened, if that ever comes. The GM knows. The character doesn't.

**For everyone:**

Dreams change. Not necessarily violently — persistently. Images with no referent in lived experience. Sensations of wrong scale. The sense, difficult to articulate, that the normal proportions between things are not the true proportions.

This is not a mechanical debuff with a duration. It is a permanent narrative consequence that the GM and player manage together over time.

## The Lich and Religious Beliefs

A region living in proximity to a lich — even without knowing it, even interpreting its presence as something completely different — develops over time rituals, taboos, practices that instinctively manage coexistence with that distortion.

Some work. Not because those who developed them understood what they were doing — but because centuries of empirical selection eliminated practices that led to worse consequences and maintained those that allowed survival.

Some are completely wrong but survive because those who questioned them died for unrelated reasons, or because the social structure maintaining them is too consolidated to be challenged.

A priest with enough Wisdom might read the difference between useful ritual and useless ritual. They probably don't say this out loud.

The cosmologies that arise around a lich tend to attribute intention, judgment, interest in human beings to it. This is almost certainly false — but it is the only framework a human mind has for processing something of that scale. Attributing intention is more manageable than accepting absolute indifference.

---

## Final Note

Destroying a lich is like destroying reality, or god, or love. It is not an objective given by anyone of sound mind. Those who propose it as a mission are reading a centuries-old tome written by someone who was themselves interpreting a legend, without understanding the scale difference between what is described and what a human being can actually do.

The sensible mission is never destruction. It is survival. It is partial understanding. It is leaving the encounter with something — information, an object, a testimony — that no living human possesses.

And bringing it back.
